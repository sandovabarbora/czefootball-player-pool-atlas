## Bugs found and fixed (critical read → code fixes, not just copy)
Reading the England output surfaced three real computational bugs, all pre-existing in nation-unaware code that 14a/14b's literal `"CZE"`-string sweep didn't catch because the bugs weren't string literals — they were **unfiltered shared config dicts** and **one stale hardcoded YAML value**. All three are fixed in `src`, covered by new tests, and verified to leave the Czech run's numbers byte-for-byte unchanged (cross-checked against `docs/index.html`'s already-published numbers before touching anything).

1. **Exhibit A (youth exposure) leaked Czechia's peer countries into the England page.** `config/leagues.yaml`'s `peer_domestic:` block (SVK/AUT/HUN/POL/CRO/DEN/SUI/NOR — Czechia's peers, none of whom has a domestic league *inside* Europe's top-9) is one shared, nation-independent registry. `src.pathways.build_pathways()` read it unfiltered, so England's Exhibit A ("share of a domestic league's minutes played by its own U-21s") showed eight irrelevant countries' bars (Slovakia, Austria, Hungary, Poland, Croatia, Denmark, Switzerland, Norway) — none of them England's actual peers (FRA/GER/ESP/ITA/NED/POR/BEL) — alongside England's own Premier League bar. **Fix**: `build_pathways()` now restricts `peer_domestic` to entries whose country is in the current run's own `peers`. For Czechia this changes nothing (all 8 registry entries are its own peers); for England it correctly reduces to *only* the Premier League's own row. New test: `test_build_pathways_drops_peer_domestic_leagues_of_non_peer_countries`.
2. **The same leak, independently, in `destinations()` (Exhibit E)** — a second, separate read of the same unfiltered registry classified an English player abroad in, say, Poland's Ekstraklasa as `"peer_domestic"` (implying Poland is an England peer, which it isn't) instead of `"other"`. `destinations()` now takes a `peers` parameter and filters the same way. New test: `test_destinations_peer_domestic_league_of_a_non_peer_country_falls_to_other`.
3. **`config.leagues()["domestic"]` was a stale `"CZE-First League"` literal, unrelated to `config.DOMESTIC_LEAGUE`.** `config/leagues.yaml`'s own `domestic:` key predates Task 14a's nation-configuration refactor and was never updated — it's a *different* mechanism from the nation-aware `config.DOMESTIC_LEAGUE` (`nation()["domestic_league"]`) that 14a correctly wired everywhere else. Under `NATION=eng`, every caller that read `leagues()["domestic"]` silently computed against **Czechia's** domestic league instead of England's:
   - `pathways.destinations()`'s tier bucketing (`league == domestic_league → "domestic"`) checked against `"CZE-First League"`, so no English Premier League row was *ever* recognised as `"domestic"` — every eligible English player showed up as `"abroad"` (`n_abroad == n_total`, i.e. **100% "abroad"**, before the fix).
   - The `sideways` definition's threshold multiplier came from `mult["CZE-First League"]` (0.434) instead of the Premier League's own 1.0 (the *highest* multiplier in the whole registry) — this produced `sideways_share = 6.25%` before the fix, which made no sense once you know the Premier League's multiplier is the ceiling (nothing meaningfully "outranks" it, so the correct number should be close to 100%, not close to 0%). This was the single number that gave the bug away.
   - `render.py`'s `_country_sideways_share` (slide 8's own-nation column) had the identical problem.
   - Discovered via exactly this contradiction: a "6.25% moved sideways" headline number, checked against the Premier League's actual position (rank 1 of 19 by multiplier) — mathematically impossible for that number to be small once the domestic league's own multiplier is the ceiling.
   - **Fix**: `config.leagues()` now overrides `cfg["domestic"]` with `nation()["domestic_league"]` at the single choke point, so every caller gets the right value without a per-call-site patch. Verified against `nation()`, not the module-level `DOMESTIC_LEAGUE` constant, because `leagues()` is called at import time (by `HEADLINE_LEAGUES`) before that constant exists. New test: `test_leagues_domestic_key_tracks_the_home_nation_not_the_yaml_literal`.
   - **After the fix**: England's destinations are **48 of 144 abroad** (not 144 of 144), and **sideways_share = 100%** (not 6.25%) — the real, meaningful number: since the Premier League has the highest league-quality multiplier of any league on file, *any* English player who leaves it for another top-9 league is, by the report's own definition, moving sideways or down. This is now a genuine, correctly-computed finding, not an artefact.
4. **A TOC leftover.** The sidebar contents list's slide-8 entry (`"toc.q8"`) was a hardcoded `"Norway and Denmark"` (English) / `"Norsko a Dánsko"` (Czech) literal — never parameterised with the `{a}`/`{b}` compare-country placeholders that `slide.8.q`'s own heading already used, so it displayed the wrong pair on the England page ("Norway and Denmark" instead of "France and Spain") even after the rest of the fixes. Fixed by hoisting `pc_a`/`pc_b` (`term(peer_compare.names[...])`) to the top of `templates/report.html.j2`, so both the early TOC entries and the later slide-8 section share the same computed names; `toc.q8` is now `"{a} and {b}"` / `"{a} a {b}"`.

## Reading the England output critically (the brief's checklist)

### Per-capita, when the home league is one of the nine
England's Premier League *is* one of the nine headline leagues, so — unlike Czechia — the vast majority of English top-9 players never left home to get there; per-capita counts them anyway (the metric is "on a top-9 roster", not "abroad"). This is why England has the *most* raw top-9 players (208, more than any of its 7 peers) yet ranks only 6th of 8 by rate: it needs proportionally far more of them, given a population 3–8x any single peer's, just to match a small nation's density. The `{home_note}` conditional (Task 14b) correctly fires and explains this on slides 4/5 ("Premier League is itself one of Europe's top-9 leagues; here 'abroad' means the other 8").

### Exhibits A–F
- **A (youth exposure)**: genuinely degenerate for England, *correctly* now (post-fix) — only England's own Premier-League bar shows (3.0% U-21 share); no peer bars at all, because none of England's seven real peers (FRA/GER/ESP/ITA/NED/POR/BEL) has a domestic league that sits *outside* the top-9 the way every one of Czechia's peers does. This is a structural fact about England's peer cohort, not a bug post-fix, and it's worth flagging: the exhibit's whole "peer domestic league" concept was designed around Czechia's situation and has nothing to show for a nation whose peers are all themselves top-9 countries.
- **B (export route)**: `origin_shares.domestic` is **0% for all eight cohort countries** (England and every one of its peers), `not_covered` is 95–100% for six of the eight. This is *not* the same bug as (1)/(2) above (already fixed) — it's a second, deeper structural fact: `export_route`'s "domestic" origin bucket requires a player's pre-top-9 season to have been played in a *separate* domestic league, distinct from top-9 — impossible when a player's first senior minutes *are* their first top-9 minutes (true for England and, in this specific cohort, every one of its peers too, since all eight nations' own top-flights are headline leagues). The exhibit's premise ("how old were they, and by which door did they leave home") doesn't have an answer when there's no door to walk through — this is a genuine, not fixable-by-config, limitation of the exhibit design for a headline-domestic-league nation; documented here rather than coded around (would need redefining what "export origin" means for this cohort, out of this task's scope).
- **C (how exports fare)**: reads fine — minutes share and club-strength percentile are computed the same way regardless of whether "abroad" means "genuinely abroad" or "another top-9 league"; England's number (33% of club minutes) is directly comparable to its peers'.
- **D (profile by tier)**: same structural collapse as B — no `"domestic"` tier rows appear for *any* of the eight countries (`tiers present: {stepping_stone, top9, other}`), for the identical reason: `headline` is checked before `domestic` in the tier classifier, and every cohort member's own league is headline.
- **E (destinations)**: now correct post-fix (see bug #3) — 48 of 144 abroad, 100% sideways, a real and interesting number precisely because the Premier League is the strongest league in the registry.
- **F (WC squad by tier)**: 96–100% "top9" tier for every one of the eight squads (England's own squad included) and **0% "domestic" for all of them** — same root cause as B/D (headline checked first), but here it's *harmless* in practice: `squad_lens`'s `peer_domestic` dict is unfiltered too, but since none of its values ever equal any of the England-cohort's own country codes, it can never falsely tag a squad member "domestic" — verified this doesn't need the same fix as (1)/(2) (checked squad_lens.py's `main()` explicitly, its home-league mapping already correctly used `config.DOMESTIC_LEAGUE`, not `leagues()["domestic"]`, so bug #3 never touched it).

Bottom line: exhibits A, B, D and F's "own domestic league vs. top-9" framing is close to meaningless for England's specific cohort (all 8 nations are themselves headline-league countries) — this was true even before any bug fix and remains true after; it's a property of the chosen peer set, not something a code change fixes. C and E are meaningful and, after the fixes above, now correct.
