Five pathway measures: Denmark, Norway and Czechia, 2025/26

Five measures, from youth minutes at home to players in Europe’s top-ranked leagues, for Danish football and the two comparison countries used throughout the atlas. Each links to the page with the full evidence. The order follows a player’s path; it is not a causal chain.

  1. Playing time for under-21 nationals in the country’s own top league.

    Share of league minutes that went to players aged 21 or under

    DEN 15.3 % NOR 11.5 % CZE 6.4 %

    Clubs that gave those young players more than a tenth of their own playing time

    DEN 11 of 12 clubs NOR 12 of 16 clubs CZE 9 of 16 clubs

    Under-21 nationals who were regular starters, per club in the league

    DEN 2.50 per club (30 players) NOR 1.56 per club (25 players) CZE 1.06 per club (17 players)

    The regular-starter line is drawn at ten starts; at five it is 3.2 per club, at fifteen 1.6 (ten: 2.5).

    They are not used mainly as late substitutes: 14.6 % of the league’s starts went to them against 15.3 % of its minutes, and a starting under-21 was on the pitch for a median 77 minutes against the league’s 79.

In the gap decomposition (descriptive, see its page), the channel with the largest contribution is how strong the domestic league is for Norway and how much playing time young players get at home for Czechia. It describes associations across 8 countries and does not say what would change the gap.

What this means

The five measures are associations, measured the same way for Denmark, Norway and Czechia and placed in the order of a player’s path. The order is not a causal chain: the page shows where Denmark differs from the two comparison countries, and it does not show which measure, if changed, would change the per-head count. Samples differ by measure and are given with each number.

What this does not show

  • Money: transfer fees, wages and academy budgets play no part in any number above.
  • How the academy and coaching set-up actually work day to day, which no public data source used here can see.
  • Agents, and how a move abroad actually gets arranged, which happens off any table this pipeline can read.
  • The direction of the arrow: a low share of playing time for young players at home could help cause a thin generation, or just as easily be a symptom of one — the five numbers above are associations measured the same way for every country.
