Summary · Question 9 of 14

Danish players with 450+ minutes in the Big-5 leagues per season, 1995/96 to 2025/26

Danish players with ≥ 450 Big-5 minutes numbered 38 at the 2025/26 peak, 13 at the 1995/96 low and 38 in 2025/26.

The change-point model’s most probable season for the rise is 2021/22 (posterior 23 %), a level change of ×1.41 (90 % HDI 0.88–1.97). The 90 % interval for the rise includes ×1.00, so the rise is not distinguishable from no change at that level.

Change-point fit: R-hat ≤ 1.004, minimum bulk ESS 372, 3 divergent transitions; a small number of divergences can bias the posterior slightly and is disclosed rather than tuned away.

How we know

As an analytics question In numbers: Danish players with ≥ 450 minutes in the Big-5 leagues each season since 1995/96, with each step in the level that a change-point model can date.

What we did We counted, season by season, how many home-nation players had real playing time in Europe's five biggest leagues, then asked a change-point model for the most probable seasons of a lasting rise and a lasting fall, with a probability on each.

Sources FBref Big-5 player tables 1995/96 → 2025/26; peers on the same rule. The names are, for each of the three seasons with the highest counts, the Danish players with the most Big-5 minutes that season, goalkeepers included, listed as a record of presence and not as a quality ranking: 2025/26: Victor Nelsson, Morten Frendrup, Joachim Andersen; 2024/25: Morten Frendrup, Mikkel Damsgaard, Christian Nørgaard; 2022/23: Kasper Schmeichel, Pierre Højbjerg, Rasmus Nicolaisen. The dates come from a Bayesian local-level model with two ordered change points, one for the rise and one for the fall (Adams and MacKay, 2007); detail in § Methodology.

What this means

The model’s most probable season for the rise is 2021/22 (posterior 23 %; level ×1.41, 90 % HDI 0.88–1.97). A break model describes the timing of a change in level; it identifies no cause, and the posterior says how sure the model is of the season. The forecast is the one registered item in the atlas and is scored only when the season’s tables are final.

What this does not show

Players in leagues outside the Big-5 (the top-9 count on the per-head page is wider), why the level changed, and any season beyond the one registered forecast.
